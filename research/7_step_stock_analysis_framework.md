# 7-Step Framework for Analyzing Stocks Using AI

This document outlines a structured, 7-step process for fundamentally analyzing stocks (with a focus on Indian markets) utilizing AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT. This methodology reduces a task that normally takes 2-3 days into a few hours, while ensuring high accuracy and depth.

## Step 1: Understand the Business and Industry
Before looking at financial ratios, you must understand exactly how the company makes its money.
* **Identify Income Sources:** Break down the revenue streams. Where is the cash actually coming from?
* **Assess Business Quality:** Not all revenues are equal. For example, Pharma companies often have better cash flows with timely payments. In contrast, EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) or government contractors often have their final 10% payments stuck for years, indicating a lower quality of cash flow.
* **Action with AI:** Prompt the AI to break down the company's business model, its product segments, and the geographic distribution of its sales.

## Step 2: Evaluate the Management (Track Record & Integrity)
A great business run by dishonest management is a bad investment.
* **Guidance vs. Delivery:** Ask the AI to compare what the management promised in historical earnings calls (concalls) versus what they actually delivered. 
* **Spotting Red Flags:** If management promises 25-30% growth during a quarter, but delivers negative 20% growth shortly after (e.g., Valiant Organics example), they are likely misleading investors. Such stocks should go straight to a blacklist. 
* **Action with AI:** Create a detailed table mapping "Year/Quarter -> Management Guidance -> Actual Outcome".

## Step 3: Identify Growth Triggers
You need to know what will drive the stock price and business growth in the next 1 to 3 years.
* **Feed Latest Data:** Do not rely purely on the AI's internal internet search, as it might pull outdated articles to save computing tokens. Always download and manually upload the latest 3-4 concalls and investor presentations to the AI.
* **Quantify the Narrative:** AI loves to tell stories. Force it to attach numbers to the narrative. If the company acquired a new business (e.g., an IVF medicine startup), ask the AI: "How much will this contribute to the top line? What are the expected margins?"

## Step 4: Analyze the Competition
AI will often list a generic set of competitors. It is your job to filter the noise.
* **Direct vs. Indirect Competition:** A company might technically be in the same sector, but not a direct competitor. For example, one company might produce patented cancer drugs, while another makes generic variants that just went off-patent. 
* **Action with AI:** Ask the AI to compare the specific sub-segments and market share of the target company against the listed competitors to see if they genuinely threaten each other's market share.

## Step 5: Assess the Risks
Identify what could destroy your investment thesis.
* **Industry-Specific Risks:** For Pharma, the biggest risks are regulatory approvals (like USFDA clearances). 
* **Client & Geographic Risks:** Check if the business relies heavily on lumpy exports, specific contract manufacturing (CDMO) clients, or geopolitical dependencies (e.g., raw materials from China).
* **Keyman Risk:** Particularly in family-driven businesses, disputes between promoters or heavy reliance on a single founder can be a massive risk.

## Step 6: Calculate Valuation Scenarios
This step should be done *after* the primary business report is generated. Feed the complete business analysis back into the AI as context for a separate valuation prompt.
* **Scenario Planning:** Have the AI calculate expected growth, margins, and valuations across three distinct scenarios:
  1. **Base Case:** The most likely outcome based on realistic assumptions.
  2. **Bull Case:** The aggressive, best-case scenario if all growth triggers fire perfectly.
  3. **Bear Case:** The worst-case scenario. This tells you your maximum downside risk (e.g., identifying that even in the worst case, you might only face a -4% CAGR loss over 2 years, making the risk acceptable).

## Step 7: Cross-Question the AI (Don't Follow Blindly)
The final and most crucial step. The AI is an assistant, not the ultimate decision-maker.
* **Interrogate the Output:** When you read the generated report, questions should naturally arise. If the AI mentions an acquisition for 100 crores, ask it: "What was the revenue of the acquired company over the last 5 years?" or "Is the new product higher or lower margin than the base business?"
* **The 90/10 Rule:** The AI does 90% of the heavy lifting—gathering data, structuring reports, and summarizing concalls. You must apply the final 10% of human curiosity, forensic checking, and critical thinking to make the actual investment decision.
